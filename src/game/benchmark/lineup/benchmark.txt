# The benchmark: one session of the game, in sections. See src/game/benchmark/Benchmark.cs for the format
# and src/game/benchmark/Directives.cs for the @ directives; other commands are the game's console commands.
#
# The tour of the worlds is one flight of the camera (src/game/benchmark/Path.cs): it passes through each @key at its
# time, turning, climbing and turning again as it goes, from Earth to Jupiter without a cut. Every @shot is a
# checkpoint: the camera and the simulation ease to a stop, the game catches up with the script's game time and the
# screenshot is taken, so the screenshots show the same moments on every Mac. benchmark.sh --explore captures keys
# from the game.
#
# A number after a screenshot's name is how much of it may change from run to run (percent of its blocks), for views that
# physics, particles or the clouds and sea change every time: about three times what repeated runs showed. A view of the flight
# in fast mode stands paused and comes out the same to the pixel, so there it doesn't count.
#
# Three tests, and "full", which plays them one after the other. The sections each one plays, in fast mode (benchmark.sh
# --fast) and in the cinematic run, split by "|" (benchmark.sh and the game's Mac Port tab read these lines): the Planetary
# Tour, whose fast mode is the flight's places with the camera jumping between them; the Mixed Test, what the tour doesn't
# check, whose fast mode is the mixed section; and the Warp Test, one way to play in both.
test tour earth moon mars saturn jupiter | earth moon mars saturn jupiter
test mixed mixed | capsule launch giga
test warp warpclean warpdebris warpfalling | warpclean warpdebris warpfalling

# Stress: the bundled "giga rocket" (about 2,000 parts: 200 solid boosters on eight curved arms, hundreds of batteries
# and lights) on launch pad OLM A at Starbase. The camera turns slowly around it all along, starting close and slowly
# rising and pulling back: the pad, the ignition and lift-off (200 plumes, many lights, a spiral of smoke), the rocket
# blown up whole, the fire and smoke seen from above. No checkpoint between the ignition and the explosion: the
# simulation slowing down or stopping makes the game's g-load check break the rocket up at a moment that differs from
# run to run.

# Mixed, the Mixed Test's fast mode: the vehicles' views at one moment of the game, the game paused. The camera comes in
# from 40 m to a kitten in its helmet floating in orbit, its eyes on the camera, Earth going by behind, while everything is
# set up (@kitten, @front holds the camera on it, zooming by the game's clock): each view has a vehicle of its own, placed
# and started so that its moment comes at the same game time: the giga rocket blown up whole as it lights its 200 boosters
# on its pad at Starbase, a rocket rising from LC-39A, one high over the coast from Vandenberg (put in the air with @fly, so
# it skips its climb), and a capsule's cockpit in orbit by the kitten, the sun coming in through one window onto its wall
# and Earth in the other (@fly, @face, @fov; the patch of sunlight needs IVA ray tracing on). A vehicle's throttle starts at
# full, so a rocket starts with its engines and the staging key alone. Then the game pauses and the camera goes from view
# to view. The clouds start from the same place every run (@time), and each start and blast waits for the game's clock
# (@sync), so it happens at the same game time on every Mac.

# The Warp Test: a capsule in a 400 km orbit asked for a speed at once, as a player holding the warp key does. Live
# sections: each part takes the same time whatever the game gets through in it, starts from a new game's world, and keeps
# its results when a later one ends the run. warpclean: the ship alone, over Earth at a speed where the sun rises three
# times a second (what the clouds' shadows cost in low orbit at warp), then at the top speed. warpdebris: 18 rockets blown up around Earth in
# orbits from 150 to 800 km. warpfalling: 18 blown up high, falling back, their pieces landing.
# What is around a ship doesn't depend on the camera, and in low orbit at the top speed day and night would flash by every
# frame: at that speed the camera stands on the Moon, zoomed in on Earth over its horizon, and stands there again for the screenshot, the Moon
# having moved on from under it. The screenshots after a warp differ from run to run (the game's clock is wherever it got
# to), so they are there to look at.
