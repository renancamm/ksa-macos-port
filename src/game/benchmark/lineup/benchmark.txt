# The benchmark's lineup: its three tests and the scenarios they play. The format and the kinds are in
# src/shared/benchmark/Lineup.cs, the directives (the steps starting with @) in src/shared/benchmark/Directives.cs; other
# steps are the game's console commands.
#
# The Planetary Tour is one flight of the camera (src/game/benchmark/Path.cs): it passes through each @key at its time,
# turning, climbing and turning again as it goes, from Earth to Jupiter without a cut. Every @shot is a checkpoint: the
# camera and the simulation ease to a stop, the game's clock lands on the script's game time and the screenshot is taken,
# so the screenshots show the same moments on every Mac. benchmark.sh explore captures keys from the game.
#
# How much each screenshot may change from run to run is measured, not written here: benchmark.sh calibrate keeps it in
# lineup/noise/.
test tour earth moon mars saturn jupiter
test mixed mixed
test warp warpclean warpdebris warpfalling

# The Mixed Test: the vehicles' views at one moment of the game, the game paused. The camera comes in
# from 40 m to a kitten in its helmet floating in orbit, its eyes on the camera, Earth going by behind, while everything is
# set up (@kitten, @front holds the camera on it, zooming by the game's clock): each view has a vehicle of its own, placed
# and started so that its moment comes at the same game time: the giga rocket blown up whole as it lights its 200 boosters
# on its pad at Starbase, a rocket rising from LC-39A, one high over the coast from Vandenberg (put in the air with @fly, so
# it skips its climb), and a capsule's cockpit in orbit by the kitten, the sun coming in through one window onto its wall
# and Earth in the other (@fly, @face, @fov; the patch of sunlight needs IVA ray tracing on). A vehicle's throttle starts at
# full, so a rocket starts with its engines and the staging key alone. Then the game pauses and the camera goes from view
# to view. Each start and blast waits for the game's clock (@sync), so it happens at the same game time on every Mac, and
# the setup runs the game in steps of the same size on every Mac. The test starts in a game of its own and goes from view
# to view in the same order, so the clouds and the sea, which change with where the camera has been, look the same every
# run.

# The Warp Test: a capsule in a 400 km orbit asked for a speed at once, as a player holding the warp key does. Timed
# scenarios: each part takes the same time whatever the game gets through in it, starts from a new game's world, and keeps
# its results when a later one ends the run. warpclean: the ship alone, over Earth at a speed where the sun rises three
# times a second (what the clouds' shadows cost in low orbit at warp), then at the top speed. warpdebris: 18 rockets blown up around Earth in
# orbits from 150 to 800 km. warpfalling: 18 blown up high, falling back, their pieces landing.
# What is around a ship doesn't depend on the camera, and in low orbit at the top speed day and night would flash by every
# frame: at that speed the camera stands on the Moon, zoomed in on Earth over its horizon, and stands there again for the screenshot, the Moon
# having moved on from under it. The screenshots after a warp differ from run to run (the game's clock is wherever it got
# to), so they are there to look at.
scenario warpclean timed
0 @reset
0 @launch benchmark-capsule at GSC ELA-4 as ship
0.2 @warp 1 0.1
0.4 @vehicle ship
0.4 @fly 400km orbit
1 @chase 215 30 30
3 @warp 16384 2
3 @measure low 8
11 @warp 0 0.5
12 @place Luna 81 0 400 177.2 2.4
12 @fov 13
12 @warp 524288 1
12 @measure clean 13
25 @warp 0 0.5
25.5 @place Luna 81 0 400 177.2 2.4
26 @shot clean
